Profile

Researcher with a Master of Architecture and postgraduate training in urban studies (expected graduation October 2026). My work sits at two ends of one question — how capital, land and infrastructure decide where things get built. One line of research examines how Chinese cross-border e-commerce capital physically landed in the Wallonia–Liège logistics node; another builds field-verified spatial data on urban human–animal relations in Brussels. Both combine documentary and market evidence — company information, planning documents, cadastral and land-registry records — with GIS, mapping and descriptive quantification. I write research reports and present in English and Chinese, and where I use generative AI I work inside a documented verification protocol: sources are returned for manual cross-checking, analytical frameworks and coding rules are set by me, and any output found to contain hallucinations is excluded.

Photography practice

Urban Wandering within Everyday Infrastructure

I walk through cities around the world with a camera, drawn to the spaces and fixtures that make up a city's everyday life: streets, stations, bridges, canals, streetlights, and the inconspicuous corners between buildings. They are rarely anyone's destination, yet they keep shaping how people meet, pause and pass through. I work with a Lumix S5 mirrorless body and a changing set of vintage lenses, using their individual colour, softness and older imaging character to observe and record the everyday in these cities. My photography does not set out to summarise a city: it keeps the light, the order and the change met by chance while walking, looking in ordinary scenes for the quiet and real connection between people and the city.
